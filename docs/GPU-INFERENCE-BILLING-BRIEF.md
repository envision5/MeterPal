# Serverless / GPU-Cloud Inference: Billing Units, Margin, and Operations

**Research date: 2026-11-06.** Vendor figures read from first-party pages on that date unless marked. **VERIFIED** = read from the vendor's own page. **INFERENCE** = my arithmetic from published numbers, or a secondary source I name.

## 1. Billing units and published rate cards

Seven units are in production. Structural point: **per-token pricing is decoupled from GPU type** — no vendor publishes an "H100 token price" ladder; GPU type enters only GPU-time and reserved SKUs.

**Replicate — per-second hardware time** (VERIFIED, [replicate.com/pricing](https://replicate.com/pricing)): CPU $0.0001/sec ($0.36/hr); T4 $0.000225 ($0.81/hr); L40S $0.000975 ($3.51/hr); A100 80GB $0.001400 ($5.04/hr); H100 $0.001525 ($5.49/hr); 2×H100 $0.003050 ($10.98/hr); 8×H100 $0.0122 ($43.92/hr). **No published B200 price**; H200 and extra multi-GPU capacity come only via "committed spend contracts."

**Modal — per-second, no minimum** (VERIFIED, [modal.com/pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)): T4 $0.000164/sec ($0.59/hr); L40S $0.000542 ($1.95); A100 80GB $0.000694 ($2.50); H100 $0.001097 ($3.95); H200 $0.001261 ($4.54); B200 $0.001736 ($6.25); B300 $0.001972. CPU $0.0000131/core-sec; memory $0.00000222/GiB-sec; volume $0.09/GiB-mo. Modal's FAQ claims **no minimum usage time per instance**, contrasting clouds where "all this time is billable" (1 min–1 hr minimums). Sandboxes run 3× base CPU/memory rate.

**fal — per-second, by runner state** (VERIFIED, [fal.ai/docs/documentation/serverless/pricing](https://fal.ai/docs/documentation/serverless/pricing)): PENDING and DOCKER_PULL **not** billed; SETUP, IDLE, RUNNING, DRAINING, TERMINATING **billed**; TERMINATED not billed; HTTP 5xx never charged; multi-GPU = `gpu_count × duration`. Rates (VERIFIED, [fal.ai/pricing](https://fal.ai/pricing)): B300 $12.99/h, GB200 $9.99, B200 $7.99, H200 $6.00, H100 $4.50, RTX PRO 6000 $4.00 — committed "as low as" $5.99/$5.89/$5.49/$2.99/$2.49/$1.99 (H100 = $0.00125/sec).

**Together — three units at once** (VERIFIED, [docs.together.ai/docs/gpu-clusters-billing](https://docs.together.ai/docs/gpu-clusters-billing)): serverless per token; GPU Clusters per GPU-hour — H100 **$1.99 preemptible / $3.99 on-demand / $3.69 (3–30d) / $3.45 (31–90d) / $3.19 (91–180d) / 181+ contact sales**; H200 $2.99/$5.99/$4.99/$4.15/$3.99; B200 $4.09/$8.19/$7.99/$7.79/$6.79; B300 $4.99/$9.99. Dedicated Model Inference bills **per minute per replica**: H100 1× $5.49/hr, B200 1× $8.99/hr, H200/GB300/B300 by quote ([docs.together.ai/docs/dedicated-endpoints/pricing](https://docs.together.ai/docs/dedicated-endpoints/pricing)). Provisioned Throughput sells **PTUs** (throughput units), token-priced, 99% uptime SLA ([together.ai](https://www.together.ai/)).

**Fireworks — per-token serverless + per-GPU-second on-demand** (VERIFIED, [docs.fireworks.ai/serverless/pricing](https://docs.fireworks.ai/serverless/pricing), [fireworks.ai/pricing](https://fireworks.ai/pricing)): cached input = 20% of input; **Priority 1.25×**; **US-only capacity 1.5×**; "Ultra-fast" previews **8×** standard; size-based fallback for unlisted models (<4B $0.10, 4B–16B $0.20, >16B $0.90, MoE ≤56B $0.50, MoE 56–176B $1.20 per 1M). **Batch = 50% off input and output. Async jobs = half Standard.** On-demand GPUs bill per GPU-second with **no start-up charge**: H100 $0.134/min ($8.00/hr), H200 $8.00/hr, B200 $0.217/min ($13.00/hr), B300 $15.00/hr, GB300 $20.00/hr; region-restricted 1.5×; idle deployments scale to zero after one hour by default; reserved GPU contracts typically run one year.

**Baseten — per minute per replica** (VERIFIED, [baseten.co/pricing](https://www.baseten.co/pricing)): T4 $0.01052, L4 $0.01414, A10G $0.02012, A100 80GB $0.06667, H100 MIG $0.0625, H100 $0.10833 ($6.50/hr), B200 $0.16633 ($9.98/hr). Billing covers time "actively deploying, scaling up or down, or making predictions" — no charge at zero replicas, but startup and scale events bill.

**DeepInfra and Groq — per-token with multiplier tiers** (VERIFIED, [deepinfra.com/pricing](https://deepinfra.com/pricing) JSON): DeepInfra exposes `rate_per_service_tier_flex` = **0.8** (20% off) and `rate_per_service_tier_priority` = **1.5** on many models, plus cached-input multipliers of 0.2–0.556. Examples: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct-Turbo $0.02/$0.04 per 1M; Gemini 3.5 Flash $0.15/$0.90; Claude Opus 5 via DeepInfra $0.50/$2.50. Also **per-second audio** ($0.005/sec), **per-image-unit** ($0.05–$0.10) and **per-second music** ($0.001/sec). Groq's card lives at [console.groq.com/docs/models](https://console.groq.com/docs/models): gpt-oss-120b $0.15/$0.60 per 1M at 500 T/s, cached input $0.037; whisper-large-v3 $0.111/audio-hour. Groq moved Llama 3.1 8B Instant and 3.3 70B Versatile to Enterprise-only on 2026-08-26 and **removed groq.com/pricing on 2026-08-11** (VERIFIED from Groq docs plus secondary trackers).

**No major platform prices LLM inference per request.** Nearest analogues are fal's and DeepInfra's per-output-unit media pricing.

## 2. The margin problem (COGS)

RunPod lists H100 SXM at **$3.99/hr Secure Cloud, $2.69/hr Community Cloud** (updated 2026-11-04, VERIFIED, [runpod.io/pricing](https://www.runpod.io/pricing)); H100 PCIe $2.89, A100 PCIe $1.79. Lambda lists 8-GPU H100 SXM at $3.99/GPU; Nebius HGX H100 rose $3.85 → $4.50 on 2026-10-01; AWS Capacity Blocks $5.19 → $5.97 (VERIFIED via [safeguard.sh survey](https://safeguard.sh/resources/blog/gpu-rental-prices-2026-h100-h200-b200-per-hour-and-why-they-move)). Market H100 on-demand list prices span roughly $2–$12/GPU-hr (same source).

**INFERENCE** — gross spread on one H100-hour vs RunPod Secure $3.99, at 100% utilization: Modal $3.95 (**~0%**), Together $3.99 (**~0%**), fal $4.50 (**13%**), Replicate $5.49 (**38%**), Baseten $6.50 (**63%**), Fireworks $8.00 (**101%**). DeepInfra's dedicated H100 at $2.20/hr ([deepinfra.com/blog/best-ai-inference-platforms](https://deepinfra.com/blog/best-ai-inference-platforms)) sits *below* RunPod, implying self-operated fleet margin, not resale margin.

**Utilization is the mechanism (INFERENCE).** A platform paying $3.99/GPU-hr and reselling at $8.00 needs `3.99 ÷ u` cost per busy hour: u = 1.0 → 50% gross margin; u = 0.8 → 25%; u = 0.5 → **0%**. McKinsey puts bare-metal-as-a-service gross margins at **55–65% before depreciation** and warns "if utilization slips below 80 percent, returns will flatline" (VERIFIED, [McKinsey — The evolution of neoclouds](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/the-evolution-of-neoclouds-and-their-next-moves)). A secondary summary of that work puts gross profit after labor, power and depreciation at **14–16%** (https://www.vcluster.com/blog/ai-cloud-providers-race-to-the-bottom-mckinsey — secondary). The platforms' defense is a software layer (batching, KV-cache management), not hardware.

## 3. Cold start and idle capacity

Four answers, all VERIFIED. **Replicate** — public models: "you only pay for the time it's active processing your requests. Setup and idle time for the model is free"; instances idle "for a few minutes." Private models and **deployments** charge setup + idle + active, but "well-tuned deployments are usually only marginally more expensive than public models" ([replicate.com/docs/topics/billing](https://replicate.com/docs/topics/billing)). **Modal** — billed during load time and processing; containers kept alive **60 seconds** after the last input by default, and that idle is billed; no minimum commitment. **fal** — idle billed at full rate, `keep_alive` defaults to **60 s**; its own break-even analysis is the cleanest public exhibit of idle leakage ([fal.ai/learn break-even](https://fal.ai/learn/serverless/dedicated-gpus-vs-serverless-inference-break-even)): H100 at $4.50/hr with `keep_alive=0` → 23.3% billed utilization, $756/mo; `keep_alive=60` → 43.3%, $1,404/mo; ≥290 s → 100%, $3,240/mo; one warm H100 = $108/day; reserved $2.49/hr = $1,792.80/mo at any utilization. **Fireworks** — on-demand deployments scale to zero after one hour by default, with no start-up charge.

**INFERENCE**: the per-token tier (Together, Fireworks, Groq, DeepInfra) inverts the burden — the provider absorbs 100% of idle and warm-pool cost, so a customer's token price implicitly funds a floor it never uses.

## 4. Billing correctness

**Replicate** (VERIFIED, [billing](https://replicate.com/docs/topics/billing), [prediction lifecycle](https://replicate.com/docs/topics/predictions/lifecycle), [terms](https://replicate.com/terms)): failed runs are not charged; a **canceled** run is charged for the time it ran; an **aborted** run (never started) is not charged; predictions time out at 30 minutes. One documented inconsistency: for models that call other models, "if the model fails, you will be billed for the duration of the run, plus the cost of the downstream models that were called before the failure occurred" — sitting awkwardly beside "for all models, if a run fails, we don't charge you." Replicate's terms make its meters authoritative: "server logs, API call records, and internal systems' measurement of compute time serve as the definitive and authoritative record"; disputes must be raised within 30 days of the due date; the customer bears the burden of proof; "failure to monitor your usage does not constitute grounds for a billing dispute."

**fal** (VERIFIED, [model-apis FAQ](https://fal.ai/docs/documentation/model-apis/faq), [serverless FAQ](https://fal.ai/docs/documentation/serverless/faq)): HTTP 5xx never charged; HTTP 422 may still be charged if GPU time was burned; Model API cold starts (pull + load) are **not** charged; Serverless time on a **failed** request *is* charged, because the runner was alive. **Fireworks**: no start-up charge; on Spot, "a request that falls back to Standard is billed at the Standard rate" — a silent price change.

**Complaints (VERIFIED as published user reports).** fal reviewers report $20 prepaid consumed with no usable video, then a balance of **−$11.20** (https://www.trustpilot.com/review/fal.ai); an r/n8n user reports a **$110 bill on a $10 credit** with negative balance, explained by "fallback to pay-as-you-go" continuing past zero (https://www.reddit.com/r/n8n/comments/1lpu7wd/unexpected_110_bill_from_falai_after_10_credit_am/); fal's tracker shows **10,272 seconds charged for an 80-second video** (https://github.com/fal-ai/fal/issues/1175); a Replicate user reports "billed for more processing time than I intended" with no support reply for a week (https://www.reddit.com/r/generativeAI/comments/1sz0gyl/). The archetypal dual-meter failure is Claude Code charging API-billed users *and* deducting prepaid credits for the same $0.26 request (https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/23315) — a design warning for any dual-path meter.

## 5. Enterprise procurement

**Together is the most explicit** (VERIFIED): org-level consolidated billing; SSO via SAML/OIDC (Google Workspace, Okta, Entra, JumpCloud) on Scale and Enterprise; projects and API keys as allocation boundaries; `GET /v1/billing/usage` returns line items with product, quantity, unit price, cost, `project_id`, `api_key_id` for internal chargeback; hard cut-off at $0 on prepaid or an **in-arrears cut-off** at an agreed negative balance for contracted enterprise accounts; auto-recharge ([billing & spend controls](https://docs.together.ai/docs/billing-spend-controls), [organizations](https://docs.together.ai/docs/organizations)). AWS Marketplace sells **prepaid Consumption Units** — 10k/25k/50k credits at $1.00 each, 12-month saving "up to 92%", 24-month "up to 96%", additional usage at $1.00/credit, fees "non-refundable and non-cancellable except as required by law" (VERIFIED, [AWS Marketplace listing](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-3zxbbifggyplc)).

**Replicate** offers prepaid-credit *and* payment-in-arrears models, early mid-month fraud-control charges credited on the next invoice, plus enterprise volume discounts, SLAs, indemnity, one contract covering 50,000+ models, and reserved compute (VERIFIED, [terms](https://replicate.com/terms), [enterprise](https://replicate.com/enterprise)). **fal** sells reserved capacity via sales as a three-part contract: a reservation floor billed every hour of the term, a burst band at a negotiated rate, further scale at per-second list. **Modal** enterprise adds volume discounts, audit logs, Okta SSO, HIPAA, VPC/on-prem (VERIFIED, [digitalocean.com/articles/ai-inference-platforms](https://www.digitalocean.com/resources/articles/ai-inference-platforms)).

Buyer-side norms (secondary but specific): reserved/commitment discounts **15–35%**, take-or-pay **30–50%+**, spot **40–70%**; buyers should demand a numeric capacity commitment and an explicit non-delivery remedy (https://gpuinsights.net/enterprise-gpu-procurement-playbook-2026/). 1–3 year commits at 30–50% off typically carry **termination fees or forfeiture of prepaid credits** on early exit (https://io.net/blog/how-to-avoid-gpu-vendor-lock-in-the-hidden-tax-on-your-ai-infrastructure).

**Where prepaid breaks (INFERENCE):** metered line-item detail is a feature of the *arrears* invoice path; on prepaid credits the customer sees only a balance delta. Together concedes it: "metering has a short delay, so some usage can arrive after the balance reaches zero. You remain responsible for that usage. The cut-off does not guarantee an exact spending cap." fal's negative-balance reports are that failure seen from outside.

## 6. Workload and model migration

The binding artifact is the **model ID**. OpenAI's policy is "at least 6 months" for GA models, "at least 3 months" for specialized variants (VERIFIED, [platform.openai.com/docs/deprecations](https://platform.openai.com/docs/deprecations)) — but GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4.1 and GPT-4.1-mini were notified **2026-10-14** and shut down **2027-01-14**: three months, a documented violation of OpenAI's own policy. The replacement `gpt-6-luna` is $0.10/$0.50 per 1M but **does not support fine-tuning**, requires the Responses API for reasoning plus tool calls, and doubles the input rate above 272k prompt tokens. Anthropic's Opus 5.5 shipped four breaking changes causing 400 errors in existing agent integrations (https://thenewstack.io/openai-gpt4o-api-shutdown/).

The deeper risk is **silent drift under a stable ID**. Anthropic has stated on the record that it "do[es] not update the model weights once they have been deployed without also changing the model ID"; users report Sonnet 3.5 v2 changing output behaviour, and statistically significant output differences between AWS Bedrock-hosted Sonnet and direct-API Sonnet **for the same model version** (VERIFIED discussion, [news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44839842](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44839842)). Google's "-latest" aliases hot-swap with two weeks' notice; xAI's retired slugs auto-redirect, changing behaviour and pricing silently unless IDs are pinned (https://vorplabs.com/models/deprecations — secondary, cites each vendor page).

On this category's platforms, **serverless model IDs are unstable by design**. Fireworks guarantees "at least 2 weeks' advance notice before removing any model" and tells production users to use on-demand deployments for "full control over model versions" (VERIFIED, [serverless overview](https://docs.fireworks.ai/serverless/overview)). Its 2026-11-07 decommissioning of GPT OSS 120B, Inkling, Nemotron and US endpoints for MiniMax M3 and GLM 5.3 Flash hits serverless only — "Dedicated deployments and Reserved Throughput are unaffected" (VERIFIED, [changelog](https://docs.fireworks.ai/updates/changelog)). Together's policy: **3-day notice for same-lineage redirects, two weeks for material changes, under 24 hours for preview models after 30 days** (https://vorplabs.com/models/deprecations, citing [docs.together.ai/docs/deprecations](https://docs.together.ai/docs/deprecations)); MiniMax M3 and Inkling shut down 2026-11-06, Qwen3.8-2.4T on 2026-11-04 (https://models.litellm.ai/deprecated-models/2026-11). Replicate's immutable versioned model IDs are the counter-design ([replicate.com/docs/topics/models/versions](https://replicate.com/docs/topics/models/versions)).

**INFERENCE for a gateway product:** `accounts/fireworks/models/deepseek-v4p1-flash` carries a 2-week removal notice and possible silent alias change; `gpt-4o` carries a 3-month notice and a $2.50/$10.00 → $0.10/$0.50 price delta; `together_ai/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-M3` had one day. A gateway that pins IDs, tracks upstream shutdown dates, and surfaces alias drift sells something the platforms themselves do not.

## 7. Spot vs on-demand

Fireworks Spot is **30% below Standard** and explicitly best-effort: "no availability or throughput guarantee, and available throughput can fall to zero"; 503 when no capacity, SDK retries twice, and **Fireworks does not fall back to Standard** — the customer must switch model IDs, and "a request that falls back to Standard is billed at the Standard rate" (VERIFIED, [spot-serverless](https://docs.fireworks.ai/guides/spot-serverless)). Published Spot rates: GLM-5.3 $0.182/$0.98/$3.08 vs $0.26/$1.40/$4.40 standard.

Together's preemptible tier is **~50% below on-demand** (H100 $1.99 vs $3.99; H200 $2.99 vs $5.99; B200 $4.09 vs $8.19) on interruptible nodes. Modal inverts the norm: **Functions are preemptible by default**, and non-preemptible mode carries a **3× multiplier** on CPU/memory list price; Sandboxes are not preemptible (VERIFIED, modal.com/pricing). Underlying spot markets: RunPod Community Cloud can be "preempted at any time without warning" (VERIFIED, [runpod.io/product/cloud-gpus](https://www.runpod.io/product/cloud-gpus)); Vast.ai interruptible is "often 50%+ cheaper" and host-reclaimable (https://www.devopsness.com/blog/gpu-cloud-comparison).

**No inference platform publishes an interruption rate.** Third-party estimates only: RunPod ~5% per 24h, Vast 5–30% by host, CoreWeave interruptible 60% discount with 30-second notice, AWS ~2-minute notice, GCP 30-second notice with a 24-hour cap (https://www.hosting-ranked.com/best-hosting-for-ai-ml-projects/, https://www.spheron.network/blog/gpu-spot-vs-on-demand-pricing/ — secondary). The arbitrage is real but one-sided: the platform sells "no guarantee" and keeps the discount; retry logic and double-billing risk are the customer's.

## What I could not verify

1. **No platform publishes gross margin or COGS.** Every margin figure here is my arithmetic from published rate cards vs. rental prices. McKinsey's 55–65% is for bare-metal rental (BMaaS), not inference resale; its 14–16% post-depreciation variant is a secondary summary, not McKinsey text I read directly.
2. **No platform publishes an idle-capacity loss or utilization rate.** Closest public numbers are fal's billed-utilization table (23.3% / 43.3% / 100%) and McKinsey's "below 80 percent, returns will flatline."
3. **Interruption rates for Fireworks Spot, Together preemptible and Modal preemption are unpublished**; only RunPod/Vast/CoreWeave/GCP/AWS third-party estimates were found.
4. **Together's serverless per-token rate card was not read first-hand** — together.ai/pricing is Webflow-rendered and my fetch truncated its tables; I verified Together's cluster and dedicated-endpoint cards from docs.together.ai instead.
5. **Fireworks' 2026-09-01 on-demand increase (+14% H100/H200, +30% B200) comes from a secondary price tracker**, not Fireworks' changelog.
6. **Anyscale's credits rate card was not read first-hand**, and **Groq's Batch API 50% discount** is secondary. Baseten's and Anyscale's per-minute rounding rules are unpublished.
7. **No first-party incident was found of a provider silently changing weights behind a serverless alias on these platforms.** Alias-drift evidence is upstream (OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI); platform-side evidence is serverless removal with 2-week (Fireworks) or 3-day (Together) notice.
8. **Egress, storage and data-transfer charges were not surveyed** — billed separately on Together (storage per GiB-month) and excluded from every comparison above.
